  • Competition: RAAM Group case competition, Team CYRUS (SRCC), Round 2 deck approximately 15 slides.

  • Case focus/track: reduce customer drop-off in Year 2 of after-sales servicing at RAAM Group passenger-vehicle dealerships (especially Toyota and MG in solution development).

  • Case-reported statistic: ~60% Year-2 service drop-off/non-return. This is a RAAM cohort retention measure, NOT “60% of Indian customers switch to the independent aftermarket.” It is also NOT automatically a measured 60% transfer to other garages. Confirm the case's precise numerator, denominator, service window and eligibility if possible.

  • Original Round 1 slide (“ROOT – CAUSE ANALYSIS,” page 4 of CYRUS_SHRI RAM COLLEGE OF COMMERCE.pdf) proposed four gaps:

    1. Value — authorised service premium versus alternatives.

    2. Transparency — visibility into work, explanations, approvals, bill and progress.

    3. Convenience — booking slots, location, scheduling and effort.

    4. Downtime — delays, parts availability, turnaround and unavailability of the vehicle.

  • Original Round 1 slide ended with “higher cost + higher effort + uncertainty + service-delay gap → customer deflection.” Its wording “as warranty coverage ends” / “post-warranty” must be revised: Year 2 is not necessarily post-warranty.

  • Round 1 also displayed “30–40% cheaper at local garage” and “15% of parts orders require emergency sourcing.” Do NOT carry these forward as RAAM facts without source verification.

  • Four proposed solutions remain in the integrated strategy:

    1. Predictive Service Booking: use authorised/permissioned DMS history and, where available, consented OEM connected-vehicle data to forecast service needs, reach out proactively, suggest appointments and plan parts/capacity. An enhancement to ordinary reminders, not an assertion that OEMs lack reminders.

    2. RAAM Service Lens: clearer estimates, work/repair explanations and approvals, service-progress communication, records; build on, do not pretend to invent, OEM capabilities.

    3. RAAM Service Wallet: service credits/future-redemption mechanism to make choosing RAAM again valuable; distinguish future credit from immediate discount and model subsidy/cannibalisation.

    4. RAAM Mobility+: qualifying subsidised rental access for AMC members and free mobility support for dealership-caused service delays beyond agreed turnaround terms (subject to full feasibility and policy). Targeted disruption mitigation, not unconditional free cars.

  • Other solution and financial slides already evaluate incremental recovered revenue, implementation and feasibility; the RCA slide diagnoses why the solutions might be relevant. It does not guarantee the solutions' impact.

2. Core analytical resolution: the apparent Deloitte versus RAAM paradox

Wrong old story: Independent facilities are winning because authorised dealerships are generally worse on price fairness, service quality or transparency; therefore the entire RAAM Year-2 drop-off consists of switches to independents.

What evidence instead supports:

  1. Among surveyed Indian vehicle owners, authorised servicing is dominant and better-rated on multiple service-experience parameters.

  2. Nonetheless, RAAM has a dealership-specific customer non-return challenge. National authorised-channel participation says nothing directly about loyalty to an individual outlet.

  3. Customers not returning to RAAM can:

    • (A) use a different Toyota/MG authorised dealership;

    • (B) use a local independent garage;

    • (C) use an organised independent multi-brand network (e.g. myTVS, GoMechanic, Bosch Car Service, depending on its actual OEM relationship);

    • (D) defer service / have no service due, sell vehicle, relocate or cease to be trackable.

  4. Deloitte does not report this breakdown for RAAM; the slide must not invent shares for those pathways.

  5. High customer satisfaction and strong trust in the authorised channel can coexist with weak retention at one specific dealership. Relationship/engagement continuity may be part of the problem even without poor technical workmanship.

  6. Key strategic framing: “Convert the authorised dealership's trust advantage into repeat, profitable service visits at RAAM.”

  7. In a single line: The issue is not necessarily a lack of trust in authorised servicing; it is the failure to automatically translate that trust into continued loyalty to the same dealership.

Why this is judge-proof: It distinguishes the channel (authorised vs independent), provider choice (this outlet vs another), experience (satisfaction), cohort retention (Year-2 return) and causal hypotheses (frictions). These are different constructs.

3. Grounded evidence — Deloitte's 2026 Global Automotive Consumer Study, India

Primary source: Deloitte, 2026 Global Automotive Consumer Study: Key findings — Indian market (published February 2026; online panel fielded October–November 2025; total India survey n = 1,501; n varies by question).

Official PDF: https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone1/in/en/docs/industries/automotive/2026/in-automotive-deloittes-2026-global-automotive-consumer-study-final.pdf

Use PDF's printed page numbers below (PDF zero-index page number is one less).

3A. Servicing channel — printed p. 34, Q16, n=1,048

  • 84% last serviced at an authorised/new-vehicle dealer.

  • 13% at an independent/aftermarket facility.

  • 3% serviced the vehicle themselves.

  • Meaning: distribution of most recent service channel among Indian respondents. NOT national audited market share; NOT the proportion returning to the same dealer; NOT RAAM data.

  • Terminology: Deloitte reports a broad “Independent/aftermarket facility” category. Conceptually it can encompass standalone local garages and organised multi-brand facilities, but the published chart does not disaggregate these or define every individual provider included. Do not call all 13% myTVS/GoMechanic.

3B. Service experience — printed p. 35, Q18; authorised n=881, independent n=137

Percent rating good/very good on selected parameters: | Parameter | Authorised | Independent | |---|---:|---:| | Clarity of explanations for service work | 89% | 78% | | Quality of work performed | 88% | 82% | | Fairness of price paid for work performed | 85% | 77% | | Professionalism of service staff | 89% | 79% |

  • Therefore do NOT describe independents as demonstrably better on service quality, transparency or perceived price fairness.

  • A claim that independent networks “position themselves around competitive prices, standardisation and convenience” is an offering/positioning claim, not proof of a superior achieved service experience.

  • Cross-sectional non-random channel groups may differ in customer and vehicle characteristics; these comparisons do not prove causality.

3C. Vehicle ownership-duration / age mix — printed p. 35, Q10

Distribution within each service channel: | Group | 0–3 years | 4–5 years | 6+ years | Reported average | |---|---:|---:|---:|---:| | Authorised (n=881) | 57% | 29% | 14% | 3.6 years | | Independent (n=137) | 48% | 24% | 28% | 4.7 years |

  • The report calls this “vehicle age,” but the underlying survey Q10 asks how long respondent has owned, leased or subscribed to the primary vehicle. Be careful about equating ownership tenure to exact vehicle age.

  • Valid inference: Independent-channel users have a relatively older/longer-tenure mix; authorised dealers still service many older vehicles.

  • Invalid inference: “28% of all 6+-year cars go independent,” “independents dominate older vehicles,” “switching occurs at Year 2,” or “the same customers migrated to independents as vehicles aged.”

  • A rough inversion of group counts suggests authorised still represents the majority among the 6+ group in this sample; do NOT overplay inferred proportions given rounding, survey weights and DIY exclusion.

  • These are channel-conditional distributions, not longitudinal switching probabilities.

Mapping to our four-gap importance framework:

  • Transparency: 22% transparency of price and work; another 9% explanation of work, and 8% communication relate to information but cannot be carelessly combined and labelled a measured “transparency gap.” Categories are mutually exclusive top choice responses with concept overlap.

  • Value: 10% cost/price is the single top service-experience aspect. Does NOT mean 90% do not care about price. Price fairness experience ratings are strong for the authorised channel.

  • Convenience: 6% location + 5% appointments + 4% online booking; 8% check-in/out is also process convenience. These are discrete single-choice categories; if aggregated, label the exact constructed grouping and never claim dissatisfaction or proven defection.

  • Downtime: 12% speed and 3% loaner car; a loaner is a relatively narrow highest priority and might be very valuable for a subset of disrupted/high-usage customers. Do not claim 3% is total demand for mobility.

  • The four gaps are a team-defined hypothesis architecture informed by consumer priorities. The Deloitte numbers show priority, not an incidence of problems.

3E. Trust — printed p. 37, Q22, n=1,048

Respondents' most trusted relationship:

  • 31% the dealership where they normally service their vehicle.

  • 27% the dealership where they acquired it.

  • 27% vehicle manufacturer/brand.

  • 12% finance provider.

  • 2% none.

  • Deloitte describes ~58% as trusting dealerships the most (31+27, combining two distinct categories). This does not mean 58% trust RAAM or the same specific dealership.

3F. Scope and correct attribution

Deloitte conducted the primary survey. Team CYRUS uses it as secondary research, not a survey our team conducted. The survey covers Indian respondents, not verified RAAM customers, and no specific Toyota Fortuner/MG-only subset has been established. It supports contextual relevance, NOT causal proof of RAAM-specific issues or solution effectiveness.

4. How to reconcile the macro slide's “Emerging White Space”

Industry concept: Automotive Independent Aftermarket (IAM) is broad: parts, distributors, workshops and service networks. The supplied international/Germany aftermarket value-chain graphic is a taxonomy illustration, not empirical India/RAAM evidence. At service-workshop level:

  • Local/standalone independent garages: typically price/proximity propositions; experience varies by workshop.

  • Organised independent multi-brand service networks: companies/networks aiming to standardise processes, offer packaged pricing, digital estimates/approvals, pickup/drop, tracking and multi-brand access. Some may also partner with OEMs; classify specific outlets/agreements accurately.

  • OEM-authorised dealerships: OEM-backed processes, marque expertise, genuine-parts access and usually better-rated experience in the Deloitte Indian study.

Do not show a Venn with independent networks as proven better than authorised dealers. A Venn may depict the market positioning the networks are attempting: affordability/proximity + process standardisation/digital convenience. “Emerging competitive proposition,” not “already superior across all dimensions.”

Recommended macro-slide takeaway: “Organised multi-brand networks are widening customers' servicing options as vehicles mature, even as authorised dealerships retain the strongest reported trust and service experience.”

Why this matters for RCA: The presence of alternatives makes provider choice more active, but is not evidence that 60% RAAM non-return transferred to IAM.

6. How each solution follows from the revised diagnosis

  1. Predictive Booking → relationship continuity and convenience.

    • Hypothesis: proactively contacting eligible customers using available service history and scheduling capacity reduces booking friction and missed service opportunities.

    • The innovation is forecasting and operational synchronisation, not merely sending service reminders.

    • Check current Toyota/MG OEM DMS reminder and telematics arrangements before promising data integration.

  2. Service Lens → reinforcing an existing strength (trust/transparency).

    • Deloitte already rates authorised explanation clarity well; the proposition should be consistent, proactive, digitally accessible customer experience at RAAM, over and above existing OEM features.

    • Do not state that Toyota/MG lack estimates or updates.

  3. Service Wallet → dealership-specific reason to return, not proof independents are better.

    • Tie credits to repeat RAAM visits while modelling price premium, take-up, unused credits, redeemed credits, and discounts for customers who would have returned anyway.

    • Do not infer that 10% choosing “cost/price” means only 10% value incentives.

  4. Mobility+ → targeted high-friction situations.

    • Address parts delays/overruns and eligible AMC service visits; a loaner is not the top need for every owner.

    • Deloitte's 3% loaner figure is a share naming it the single most important factor, not overall demand. Pilot the offer with customers for whom downtime is truly disruptive.

None of these links establishes causal lift. Financial slides will demonstrate viability under explicitly stated scenario assumptions; a real pilot must validate actual incremental retention.

7. Claims to avoid — mandatory guardrails

DO NOT write:

  • “60% of RAAM customers move to local garages and organised aftermarket.”

  • “Authorised dealerships are inferior to independent garages in trust/service quality/price fairness.”

  • “22% of customers suffer from transparency problems”; Deloitte asked what mattered most.

  • “12% experience service delays” or “3% need rental cars”; these are top-aspect preferences.

  • “28% of all older cars shift to independent facilities”; 28% is the 6+ ownership-tenure share within the independent sample.

  • “Customers switch after warranty ends” as a universal rule, or Year 2 always means post-warranty.

  • “Deloitte proves our four solutions will reduce non-return from 60% to 40%.”

  • “We conducted the Deloitte primary survey.” Deloitte conducted it; CYRUS is using it as external secondary research.

  • “IAM equals myTVS or only organised multi-brand.” IAM is broader.

  • “The 30–40% local-garage discount and 15% emergency-sourcing figures are established RAAM facts” without explicit evidence.

  • “RAAM currently lacks reminders, digital approvals, OEM connected services, prepaid packages or mobility offerings.” Check what exists and frame incremental additions.

Prefer language such as: “case-reported,” “Deloitte's surveyed Indian owners,” “potential friction,” “reported top priority,” “relative representation,” “could influence,” “requires RAAM validation,” “targeted pilot,” “illustrative revenue recovery.”

10. Judge-facing Q&A — prepared arguments

Q: “If 84% service at dealerships, where is RAAM's problem?”
A: “84% is the national surveyed share of most recent service channel. RAAM's 60% measures whether a customer returns to that dealer in a Year-2 cohort. Customers may remain in authorised servicing elsewhere; the two statistics are not contradictory.”

Q: “How can you claim a transparency gap when dealers score 89% on explanation clarity?”
A: “We do not claim authorised dealerships generally perform poorly. Deloitte confirms strong execution and that transparent pricing and work is the single highest-priority service attribute. Our hypothesis is that consistent digital clarity and confidence can reinforce RAAM's strong starting position; dealer-specific gaps need validation.”

Q: “Why organise the four solutions around gaps if the root causes aren't proven?”
A: “They are a structured set of potentially actionable decision frictions supported in relevance by external customer research. The rollout is staged and instrumented to verify their causal effect and eliminate unproductive costs.”

Q: “Are independent networks clearly better?”
A: “Not on Deloitte's reported Indian service-experience ratings. They expand the customer's set of offerings—potentially price and convenience led—while OEM dealerships maintain strong trust/quality. We want RAAM to capitalise on that advantage.”

Q: “What if 60% didn't actually require a service?”
A: “We would refine the eligible denominator using time/kilometre service schedules and observed transactions before estimating recoverable non-return. Our financial model distinguishes eligible, reachable customers from gross non-return.”

Q: “What exact data would you ask RAAM for?”
A: “A de-identified cohort of Year-1 customers; eligibility/due-service date; branch and brand; Year-2 return status and timing; completed bookings; service reminders; reason codes; known cross-dealer transfers; relevant capacity/delay/parts metrics; relevant privacy and OEM permissions. Without this, destinations and causality remain hypotheses.”
